Section 1

Executive Summary

This report presents a revised donor segmentation for GoodLabs, updated from the June 2026 Slack-only draft to incorporate a 554-donor CSV export and the full database schema. The quantitative data substantially confirms the qualitative segmentation while adding accurate sizing, behavioral benchmarks, and three important corrections to the prior framing.

Five donor segments emerge from combining behavioral signals (biohacker_signal, regular_donor_signal) with acquisition data (referral sources, UTM), engagement depth (Barry conversations, action plans, profile completion), and geographic/org context. The segments differ meaningfully on JTBD, acquisition channel, and activation pattern — and require distinct messaging and product experiences.

Three corrections to the prior segmentation

  1. The "Curious First-Timer" bucket was too broad. What looked like one segment in the Slack data is actually three distinct groups with different JTBDs: health-curious Converters (biohacker signal, will re-engage if results feel meaningful), Access Seekers (free healthcare access as primary driver), and Social Responders (Instagram/TikTok–driven, low intrinsic health motivation). These require entirely different activation strategies.
  2. The Nashville retention signal was premature. Blood Assurance launched in May 2026. The 93% first-timer rate in that market is launch math — the first re-eligible cohort arrives August 2026. Repeat rate data from Nashville is not yet meaningful.
  3. The "Protocol Tracker" segment is real but concentrated. It overlaps heavily with The Optimizer in the Vitalant/CA market and doesn't warrant a separate segment at current scale. Rolled into The Optimizer and The Curious Converter depending on repeat behavior.

Key finding: 79% of donors have donated only once. The critical business question is not acquisition — it's what happens between a donor's first result and their 56-day re-eligibility window. Barry engagement (chat conversations) is the strongest leading indicator of repeat behavior in the data.

Section 2

Methodology

This revision adds two new data sources to the prior Slack-only analysis: a 554-row CSV export of all donor accounts (donation_count > 0, lab_order_count = 0) and the full Drizzle ORM database schema, which clarifies field definitions, join paths, and available behavioral signals.

Segmentation Signal Model

The CSV contains two pre-computed boolean signal fields — biohacker_signal and regular_donor_signal — that serve as the primary segmentation axis. These were cross-validated against behavioral data (biomarker counts, Barry conversations, action plans, panel selections) and acquisition data (referral sources, UTM source) to confirm the signal definitions are meaningful.

The four quadrants these two signals create (bio+reg, bio only, reg only, neither) map cleanly to the qualitative archetypes from the Slack analysis, with one exception: the "neither" quadrant (46% of donors) contains at least two distinct sub-populations — Access Seekers and Social Responders — that can be partially separated by acquisition channel and geographic market.

Org → Market Mapping

Order creation events in the #donations Slack channel contain orgName, userId, and orderReferralSource fields. By joining these to the donor CSV on userId, we confirmed that the three live orgs map to distinct geographic markets: Vitalant (CA/SF Bay Area), Blood Assurance (TN/GA), and Inova (VA/DC). This provides a clean market-level segmentation layer on top of the behavioral segments.

Limitations

The Slack–CSV join matched only 30 donors (5.4%) due to Slack retaining only recent events. The CSV is the authoritative behavioral record; Slack provides qualitative signal and acquisition attribution for a recent slice. Segment sizes for Access Seekers and Social Responders are estimates — the CSV has no insurance field, making these sub-populations difficult to separate definitively without additional data.

High confidence
Primary Motivation
Active health self-management. Uses bloodwork to track longitudinally, not to diagnose. Sees donation as complementary to — or an extension of — their existing health practice.

Cross-Cutting Behaviors

The retention cliff

79% of donors have donated only once (438 of 554). This is the defining fact of the current user base. The critical question is what distinguishes the 21% who return.

Barry engagement (total_conversations > 0) is the strongest behavioral predictor of repeat donation available in the current data. Scheduling cancellations are not a churn signal — 49% of repeat donors had at least one cancellation, meaning the willingness to reschedule is itself a positive signal.

Profile completion is very low overall

Only 13% of donors have set health goals, 12% have a blood type on file, and 9% have recorded lifestyle interventions. These numbers are highest in the Optimizer segment (29% goals, 26% blood type) and near-zero in the Social Responder and Access Seeker segments. Profile depth is both a segmentation signal and a product opportunity.

Test interest by panel type

The General panel is the most ordered by volume, but the distribution shifts dramatically by segment. Biohacker-signal donors order hormone panels (Men's Hormone, Testosterone, Cortisol), nutrient panels, and CMP at meaningfully higher rates. Vitalant donors trend toward testosterone and metabolic panels; Inova donors toward heart health and nutrient panels — suggesting different health orientations in each market that warrant different content and panel merchandising.

Referral source concentration

Instagram is the single largest acquisition channel by volume (53 donors with confirmed Instagram referral in CSV, 74 in recent #donations events). TikTok is second (34 CSV, 15 in Slack window). Friend and family referral is the highest-quality channel — it produced the fewest donors by count but the most Optimizers and Habitual Altruists.

Section 8

What the Data Suggests

  • The product's retention lever is the post-result experience, not re-acquisition. Barry engagement is the strongest repeat-donation predictor available. The 56-day window between first donation and re-eligibility is the product's most important activation window. What happens in that period — what Barry says, whether results feel actionable — determines whether a Curious Converter becomes an Optimizer or churns.
  • The Optimizer segment is acquired almost entirely through trust networks. Word-of-mouth and trainer referrals produce the highest-LTV donors. Paid social produces volume (Social Responders) but not the self-directed health trackers who generate repeat visits and panel upgrades. These are different funnels requiring different investment logic.
  • Inova's 20% repeat rate is the benchmark. At 90 donors in what appears to be a moderately mature market, Inova's 20% repeat rate is the most comparable signal to what Vitalant should be producing at scale. Vitalant at 18% is roughly in line. The Southern market (Blood Assurance) will be the real test — first data available August 2026.
  • Add-on paid tests at checkout is the most visible LTV expansion opportunity. Multiple Optimizer donors raised this independently. The panel selection step is where the highest-intent users are most motivated to upgrade. This signal appeared in both the Slack qualitative data and indirectly in the CSV (high biomarker counts suggesting breadth of interest that exceeds what's available for free).
  • The Access Seeker segment is being acquired and under-served. News and poster acquisition is reaching this segment, and 20% of care-nav users are uninsured. The product and messaging isn't optimized for this JTBD — results may be surfacing health issues with no clear path to care navigation. Care-nav integration post-result could make this the highest-social-impact segment.
Section 9

What We Are Less Certain About

  • Access Seeker vs. Social Responder split within the "neither signal" cohort. The 256 donors with neither signal are the least understood group. The Access Seeker / Social Responder split is inferred from acquisition channel and geography — not directly measured. Insurance status is the missing field that would cleanly separate these sub-populations.
  • Blood Assurance retention. 93% first-timers reflects a 6-week-old market. No conclusions about Southern retention can be drawn until August 2026 when the first eligible cohort re-presents. The acquisition channels and test preferences are clear; the retention story is not yet written.
  • Barry causality. Barry engagement correlates with repeat donation, but causality is not established. High-intent donors may engage with Barry more because they were already going to return, not because Barry caused them to return. An experiment (withholding or delaying Barry prompt post-result) would be needed to establish causality.
  • What "General panel" donors actually want. The General panel is the most-ordered by far, but it's also the default. We can't distinguish donors who actively chose it from those who accepted the default. Panel selection UX data would clarify whether test merchandising could shift more donors toward specialized panels.
  • Goals field reliability. Only 71 donors (13%) have set goals, and the field shows very low completion across all segments. It's unclear whether low completion reflects low motivation or poor UX (field not prominent enough, friction in the goal-setting flow). If it's UX, this is fixable and the field becomes a much stronger segmentation signal with improvement.
Section 10

Recommended Next Steps

  • 1
    Score all 554 donors into segments programmatically Use biohacker_signal, regular_donor_signal, referral_sources, and address_state to assign a primary segment to each donor. Build a simple scoring table. This enables cohort-level analysis of retention, panel choice, and Barry engagement by segment — turning the qualitative framework into a working analytics layer.
  • 2
    Add insurance status to the donor export The Access Seeker / Social Responder split cannot be confirmed without insurance or income data. Adding a field from the orders or user_profiles table — or connecting the care-nav form responses via email join — would allow proper sizing of the Access Seeker segment and targeted activation.
  • 3
    Track the Blood Assurance first re-eligible cohort (August 2026) The earliest May 2026 Blood Assurance donors become eligible to re-donate around late July–early August. Monitoring the re-donation rate for this cohort is the first real Southern market retention signal. Set up a tracking view now so the data is ready when it arrives.
  • 4
    Test the post-result Barry prompt timing Barry engagement is the strongest repeat-donation predictor available. An experiment varying the timing and content of the first post-result Barry message (day 1 vs. day 3 vs. day 7 after results delivery) would establish whether the prompt is causal — and, if so, when it's most effective.
  • 5
    Prototype add-on paid test selection at checkout Multiple Optimizer donors independently raised the inability to add paid tests at donation time as a friction point. This is both a retention and revenue lever for the highest-LTV segment. A mockup of the add-on flow — even just as a form — would validate demand before engineering investment.
  • 6
    Build a goals-setting prompt into the post-first-result flow Only 13% of donors have set health goals despite 100% completing onboarding. Goals are the strongest profile completion signal for Optimizer behavior. A prompted goal-setting moment in the result delivery experience (not buried in onboarding) could meaningfully increase engagement depth — and improve the segmentation signal quality.
